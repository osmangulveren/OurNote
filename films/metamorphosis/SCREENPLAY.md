# THE METAMORPHOSIS

*A short film in the manner of David Fincher*
*after the novella by Franz Kafka*

Running time: **20:00** · Aspect: 2.39:1 · Palette: sick green / sodium amber / crushed black · Red: once.

---

## Treatment note (per the `david-fincher` skill)

- **The system:** the Samsa household's economy. Gregor is its engine: salary in, debt down, rent paid. The firm is the machine behind the machine.
- **The procedure:** the alarm clock, the key in the lock, the plate pushed under the door. Every act turns on one of the three.
- **The obsession:** a magazine picture of a lady in furs, in a gilt frame Gregor carved himself. The last human thing he owns.
- **The ledger:** the father's hidden money box. The family was never as poor as Gregor was told.
- **The cold ending:** a tram in spring sunlight. The family is lighter. The system has already found its next engine: Grete.

## PART ONE — THE KEY

**3. BLACK — 2:14 (8s)**

SUPER: `I. THE KEY`

**4. INSERT — ALARM CLOCK — 2:22 (10s)**

The hands read 6:30. It should have rung at four.

SUPER: `MONDAY — 6:30 A.M.`

**5. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — MORNING — 2:32 (45s)**

Grey light. The blanket slides off. On the bed, on its back, lies GREGOR: an armoured, domed, brown body; a belly of stiff arches; too many thin legs waving helplessly.

The framed LADY IN FURS watches from the wall.

> GREGOR (V.O.)
> One morning I woke from troubled dreams and found that I had been changed, in my bed, into some kind of monstrous vermin.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> It wasn't a dream. My room was still my room. The samples were still on the table.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> My first thought was: I've missed the train.

**6. INSERT — GREGOR — 3:17 (18s)**

ECU. Legs flicker in a slat of window light. An antenna tests the air. SOUND: the drone drops half a step.

> GREGOR (V.O.)
> Something is wrong with my voice.

**7. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — THE DOOR — 3:35 (35s)**

From inside: the door to the hall, a blade of amber light beneath it. Two shadows of feet stop in the light.

> MOTHER (O.S.)
> Gregor? It's a quarter to seven. Weren't you going somewhere?
>
> GREGOR
> *(a thin, piping squeak underneath the words)*
> Yes. Thank you, Mother. I'm getting up.
>
> MOTHER (O.S.)
> ...Alright.

The shadows linger. Then slide away.

**8. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — CONTINUOUS — 4:10 (35s)**

Three doors. Three family members. Gregor, on his back, in the middle.

> FATHER (O.S.)
> *(knuckles on the left door)*
> Gregor. Gregor! What's the matter?
>
> GRETE (O.S.)
> *(soft, at the right door)*
> Gregor? Aren't you well? Do you need anything?
>
> GREGOR
> I'm ready. I'm nearly ready.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> I lock my doors at night. A habit from hotels. I have never been so grateful for a habit.

**9. INSERT — ALARM CLOCK — 4:45 (8s)**

`7:00 A.M.`

**10. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — HALL — 4:53 (25s)**

A doorbell. The family freezes in the hall as three silhouettes. The front door opens to a fourth: tall, hatted, with a cane. THE CHIEF CLERK.

SUPER: `7:10 A.M.`

> GREGOR (V.O.)
> One late morning, and the firm sends the chief clerk himself. As if the whole family were under suspicion.

**11. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — THE DOOR — 5:18 (45s)**

The blade of light under the door. Now the polished shoes of the Chief Clerk.

> CHIEF CLERK (O.S.)
> Mr. Samsa. You barricade yourself in your room. You answer in monosyllables. You cause your parents serious and quite unnecessary worry.
>
> CHIEF CLERK (O.S.)
> Your performance has been unsatisfactory for some time. There has been talk of the cash payments entrusted to you.
>
> GREGOR
> Sir — it's a dizzy spell, nothing more. I'll be on the eight o'clock.
>
> CHIEF CLERK (O.S.)
> *(quiet; to the parents)*
> Did you understand a word of that?
>
> CHIEF CLERK (O.S.)
> That was the voice of an animal.

SOUND: silence. Only the tick.

**12. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — CONTINUOUS — 6:03 (20s)**

Gregor rocks his body, once, twice — and drops off the bed. A hollow thud on the carpet.

> CHIEF CLERK (O.S.)
> Something has fallen in there.

**13. INSERT — THE LOCK — 6:23 (40s)**

ECU. A brass key. A brown jaw closes on it. The key turns — a degree at a time. A thread of brown fluid runs down the escutcheon.

CAMERA: locked. The only movement is the key.

> CHIEF CLERK (O.S.)
> Listen. He's turning the key.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> Everyone should have been cheering me on. Go on, Gregor. Keep hold of that key.

CLICK.

**14. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — GREGOR'S DOORWAY — 7:03 (30s)**

Reverse: the family, framed by the door, backlit. They see him. We don't — only his shadow falls across them.

The CHIEF CLERK says "Oh!" like wind through trees. MOTHER takes two steps forward and collapses into her skirts. FATHER clenches a fist, then weeps.

> GREGOR
> I'll get dressed. I'll pack the samples. I'll go. Sir — please — put in a good word for me at the firm.

**15. INT. STAIRWELL — CONTINUOUS — 7:33 (22s)**

The Chief Clerk retreats down the stairwell, never turning his back, then leaps several steps at once and is gone. His scream echoes up the shaft.

**16. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — HALL — 7:55 (25s)**

FATHER, huge in silhouette, seizes the Chief Clerk's cane in one hand, a newspaper in the other. He stamps. He hisses — a sound like an animal.

He drives Gregor backward toward the bedroom door.

**17. INT. GREGOR'S DOORWAY — CONTINUOUS — 8:20 (18s)**

Gregor wedges in the frame, one side scraped raw. Dark stains on the white door. A kick from behind —

He flies into the room, bleeding.

The door slams. The key turns from the other side.

**18. BLACK — 8:38 (4s)**

Silence.

## PART TWO — THE PICTURE

**19. BLACK — 8:42 (8s)**

SUPER: `II. THE PICTURE`

**20. INSERT — THE BOWL — 8:50 (32s)**

Top-down. Dusk. A bowl of milk, white bread floating in it. Gregor's favorite. An antenna dips in — and recoils.

SUPER: `DUSK`

> GREGOR (V.O.)
> Milk had always been my favorite. Now it disgusted me.

**21. INSERT — THE NEWSPAPER — 9:22 (32s)**

Top-down. GRETE's hands spread a newspaper and set out a test: half-rotten vegetables, bones in white sauce, a cheese Gregor would have called inedible two days ago. She places a basin of water. She leaves. The key turns.

> GREGOR (V.O.)
> The fresh food I couldn't stand. The rotten cheese I devoured, crying with pleasure.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> She never looked at me while I ate. Kindness, or disgust. I couldn't tell anymore.

**22. INSERT — THE MONEY BOX — 9:54 (45s)**

Top-down. A lamp. FATHER's thick fingers open a small iron cash box. Coins. Paper. A ledger in his hand.

> FATHER (O.S.)
> *(through the wall)*
> A little is left from the business. And with interest — what Gregor brought home each month, I didn't spend all of it.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> I had been working for five years to pay off a debt my father could have paid in one.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> I was happy about it. That is the part I still don't understand.

**23. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — MONTAGE — 10:39 (44s)**

Same locked angle, four times, eleven seconds each. The room changes; the angle never does.

- SUPER: `DAY 9` — a sheet thrown over the couch. A dark shape underneath.
- SUPER: `DAY 23` — the window propped open. Grete, hurried, holding her breath.
- SUPER: `DAY 41` — dust. The plate untouched.
- SUPER: `DAY 60` — trails of glue-like smear across the walls and ceiling.

**24. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — CEILING — 11:23 (28s)**

Gregor hangs from the ceiling. He lets go, drops, catches himself. Almost joy.

> GREGOR (V.O.)
> Up there, I could breathe. Up there, I was almost happy.
>
> GRETE (O.S.)
> He needs room to crawl. We'll take the furniture out.

**25. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — THE PICTURE — 11:51 (40s)**

MOTHER and GRETE wrestle the chest of drawers out. They've taken the desk he did his homework on. The walls are bare — except the LADY IN FURS.

Gregor scrambles up the wall and presses his whole body over the glass. It is cool against his hot belly.

> MOTHER
> Shouldn't we leave it as it was? So he sees nothing has changed, when he comes back to us?
>
> GRETE
> He needs space. You don't know him like I do.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> They could take everything. Not this.

**26. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — CONTINUOUS — 12:31 (18s)**

MOTHER turns. She sees the enormous brown stain on the flowered wallpaper — and it moves.

> MOTHER
> Oh God — oh God —

She drops, arms flung wide. GRETE shakes a fist at him — the first time — and runs for smelling salts.

**27. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — HALL — 12:49 (22s)**

The front door. FATHER — transformed too: a tight blue uniform with gold buttons, a bank messenger's cap, the bearing of a man who has become useful again.

> GRETE
> Mother fainted. Gregor got out.
>
> FATHER
> I knew it. I told you this would happen.

**28. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — DINING ROOM — 13:11 (35s)**

FATHER fills his pockets from the fruit bowl. He throws. Small red apples roll across the floorboards like billiard balls, clicking off one another.

One glances off Gregor's back. The next one sinks in, and stays.

SOUND: everything stops except a high, thin ring.

The apple, lodged in the brown shell, is the only red in the film.

MOTHER, in her underclothes, runs to FATHER, embraces him, begs for Gregor's life.

**29. BLACK — 13:46 (6s)**

## PART THREE — THE VIOLIN

**30. BLACK — 13:52 (8s)**

SUPER: `III. THE VIOLIN`

**31. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — LIVING ROOM — EVENING — 14:00 (30s)**

Gregor's door, left open a crack each evening now. Through it: the lamp-lit table. FATHER asleep upright in his uniform. MOTHER sewing underwear for a shop. GRETE studying shorthand and French to get a better job.

> GREGOR (V.O.)
> The apple stayed in my back. No one dared take it out.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> The family had jobs now. They'd learned they could live without me.

**32. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — DAY — 14:30 (22s)**

The CHARWOMAN — a bony old widow — opens the door wide, unafraid.

> CHARWOMAN
> Come here then, you old dung beetle. Look at the old dung beetle.

She raises a chair over him. He retreats. She laughs.

**33. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — DINING ROOM — NIGHT — 14:52 (28s)**

Three LODGERS with full beards sit at the head of the table where Gregor's father used to sit. The family eats in the kitchen. Gregor's room has become the junk room: ash cans, broken chairs, a rubbish bin.

> LODGER
> *(prodding the meat)*
> Acceptable.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> They had teeth to chew with. I was starving.

**34. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — LIVING ROOM — 15:20 (45s)**

GRETE plays the violin for the lodgers. A single lamp. Her eyes follow the notes.

SOUND: the score falls away. Only the violin — thin, sad, a little out of tune.

> GREGOR (V.O.)
> I was going to send her to the conservatory. I was going to announce it at Christmas.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> Was I an animal, if music could move me like this?

**35. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — HALL — CONTINUOUS — 16:05 (20s)**

From the dark hall, Gregor drags himself — dusty, trailing lint and hair and food scraps — into the lamplight toward her.

**36. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — LIVING ROOM — CONTINUOUS — 16:25 (20s)**

The MIDDLE LODGER sees him. Stands.

> MIDDLE LODGER
> In view of the disgusting conditions in this household — I give notice. I won't pay a penny for the days I've stayed.
>
> THE OTHER TWO
> We give notice too.

The violin slides from MOTHER's lap. A dissonant twang.

**37. INT. SAMSA APARTMENT — LIVING ROOM — LATER — 16:45 (32s)**

GRETE at the table, hand flat on the wood. Level. Final.

> GRETE
> We have to get rid of it. We've done everything humanly possible.
>
> FATHER
> If he could understand us —
>
> GRETE
> It has to go. That's the only way. If it were Gregor, he'd have understood long ago that people can't live with an animal like that. He'd have gone of his own free will.
>
> FATHER
> *(a long beat; then, quietly)*
> Yes.

**38. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — NIGHT — 17:17 (20s)**

Gregor turns, painfully slowly, and crawls back into his room. The door slams behind him. The key turns. Grete's voice, outside:

> GRETE (O.S.)
> Finally.

## CODA

**39. INT. GREGOR'S ROOM — 3:00 A.M. — 17:37 (35s)**

A tower clock strikes three. Through the window, the first grey of dawn. Gregor lies still on the bare floor, the rotten apple sunk into his back.

> GREGOR (V.O.)
> I thought of my family with tenderness and love.
>
> GREGOR (V.O.)
> I agreed with her. I had to disappear.

His head sinks. From his nostrils, a last weak breath.

SUPER: `3:00 A.M.`

**40. INT. GREGOR'S DOORWAY — MORNING — 18:12 (18s)**

The CHARWOMAN pokes him with a broom. Nothing. She throws the door open and shouts into the dark apartment:

> CHARWOMAN
> Come and look. It's croaked. It's lying there, dead as a doornail.
>
> CHARWOMAN (LATER)
> You don't have to worry about the thing next door. It's been taken care of.

**41. EXT. COUNTRYSIDE — TRAM — DAY — 18:30 (40s)**

A tram, alone except for FATHER, MOTHER and GRETE, rattles out of the city into warm spring sunlight. Overexposed, too bright — the most disturbing image in the film.

GRETE stands and stretches her young body. FATHER and MOTHER look at her, then at each other.

> MOTHER
> *(quietly)*
> She's grown so pretty.
>
> FATHER
> It'll be time to find her a good husband.

SUPER: `THEY TOOK A SMALLER APARTMENT. CHEAPER. BETTER LOCATED.`

**42. END CREDITS — 19:10 (50s)**

Scratched white type on black. SOUND: the alarm clock, ticking — then ringing, for someone else.

`THE END` — **20:00**
